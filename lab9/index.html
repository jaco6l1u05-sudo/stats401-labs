Lab 9: Geospatial Visualization with D3

This visualization uses nominal GDP estimates for the 50 largest economies in 2025. GDP is measured in billions of current U.S. dollars. Countries outside the dataset are displayed as missing data.

2025 GDP Choropleth

Color represents nominal GDP. Darker countries have larger economies.

2025 GDP Cartogram

Country area represents nominal GDP in 2025.

Design and Comparison

In the choropleth, the United States and China are most prominent because they receive the darkest colors, while other top-50 economies remain visible without overwhelming the scale. I use a logarithmic color scale because GDP is highly skewed; a linear scale would compress most countries into similar pale colors. The choropleth preserves country shapes, relative positions, coastlines, and regional context, so it best supports questions about where high-GDP economies cluster and how neighboring countries compare.

In the cartogram, area rather than geographic size represents GDP. The United States, China, Germany, Japan, and India expand, while geographically large but lower-GDP economies shrink. Small but economically important countries become easier to notice. This makes ranking and comparing global economic magnitude more immediate, but it distorts land area, boundaries, distances, and recognizable shapes. The cartogram therefore better supports questions about which economies dominate total output and how large their economic shares are.

Changing from color to area makes differences in economic size feel more dramatic. The choropleth emphasizes geographic pattern; the cartogram emphasizes magnitude. Neither view is universally better: linked highlighting helps users connect distorted countries back to their familiar locations and use the two encodings together.
